## Daily limits, learning steps, lapses, leeches, burying

**Status:** Implemented

**Evidence:** deck options, Anki-style step parser, collection-limit and queue tests. The screen also reproduces Anki's advice rather than inventing rules of its own: a review limit that cannot absorb the new cards, a learning step that outlives the interval it graduates to, Good graduating past Easy, a relearning step longer than the minimum interval, a maximum interval under half a year, an answer cap above ten minutes, an easy-days row with no normal day left, and steps of a day or more once FSRS is scheduling. Each is a warning printed under its own field and never a refusal to save, which is the line Anki draws (`lib/deckOptionsRules.ts`, `lib/deckOptionsRules.test.ts`). "Limits start from top" starts off, as Anki's `apply_all_parent_limits` does: an unconfigured collection reads a subdeck's allowance from the subdeck the learner opened rather than squeezing it again through every ancestor, and a collection that had already turned the switch on keeps it (`lib/studyRepository.scope.test.ts`)

**Next compatibility gate:** Preserve selected-deck/subdeck semantics. "New cards ignore review limit" is deliberately left on where Anki defaults it off, so the two allowances stay separate unless a learner asks otherwise

## Display/gather/sort order and learn-ahead

**Status:** Implemented

**Evidence:** six gather orders, five new-card sort orders, interday/review ordering and queue tests. All thirteen of Anki's review orders are offered, including the two retrievability orders, which appear only while FSRS is on because only then does a card carry the memory state they read (`reviewSortOrderChoices`). The scheduler decides what three of them mean, as `review_order_sql(order, timing, fsrs)` does: with FSRS on the two ease orders sort by difficulty in the opposite direction — and are relabelled for it — while relative overdueness is measured against retrievability, `-(R^(-1/decay) - 1) / (DR^(-1/decay) - 1)`, falling back to the interval measure for a card FSRS has never scheduled. A card with no memory state stands where SQLite's NULL would (`lib/queueBuild.test.ts`). A preset left on a retrievability order moves to relative overdueness if FSRS is switched off, and the ordinals still round-trip through `.apkg` (`lib/exportReviewOrder.test.ts`)

**Next compatibility gate:** Keep the queue's meaning and the dropdown's label in step whenever either changes

## FSRS

**Status:** Implemented (FSRS-6), measured against Anki 26.05 (fsrs-rs 5.2.0) on 2026-09-27

**Evidence:** `lib/fsrsAnkiParity.test.ts` replays vectors recorded from Anki 26.05's own backend
(`test/fixtures/anki-26.05-fsrs.json`, regenerated by `scripts/anki-oracle/`): the four answer
outcomes of new, learning, review and relearning cards; memory states replayed from review logs
(complete, truncated, reset, cramming, manual rows, the ignore-before cutoff); whole answers
through `answerStudyCard` on SQLite, comparing every card column, `cards.data` key and review-log
column; `fuzz_delta` for card ids up to 2^52; load-balanced days; and rescheduling after a preset
change. What that pins:

- FSRS-6 in fsrs-rs's own f32 operation order (`lib/fsrs.ts`), with the short-term floor only on
  Good and Easy, the mean reversion to an Easy start, and `memory_state_from_sm2` as upstream;
- Anki's rounding of stored states (`s` 4, `d` 3, `dr` 2, `decay` 3 decimals), which the next
  answer starts from;
- days elapsed as whole days from the last review to the next rollover, from `lrt` or else the
  newest counted answer in the log; a card answered before but without a state gets one from its
  log (or SM-2 values) when it is next shown or answered (`lib/fsrsCardInputs.ts`);
- the learning/review/relearning state machine outcome for outcome, steps in whole seconds, the
  interval a relearning card keeps, the ease each transition leaves, and empty step lists;
- Anki's randomness bit for bit (`lib/ankiRandom.ts`): ChaCha12 `StdRng` seeded with
  `card id + reps`, for review fuzz, the learning-step fuzz and the load balancer;
- the load balancer and Easy Days (`lib/loadBalancer.ts`, `lib/loadBalancerSession.ts`), built
  with the study queue and fed by each answer, as Anki does;
- the order a preset recompute visits cards in, which decides the balanced days when it
  reschedules: preset by preset, cards sorted by the length of their FSRS item with Rust 1.92's
  unstable sort, ported in `lib/rustSortUnstable.ts` (ties do not keep card order);
- review-log rows: kind (a review answered early is a filtered review), `ivl`/`lastIvl` in days
  or negative seconds, and the shifted difficulty in the ease column; `lrt` and `pos` on the card;
- a deck's own desired retention, the preset-level validation that resets an out-of-band
  retention to 90%, the ignore-before date read as midnight UTC, recomputing only the decks whose
  inputs a save changed, clearing FSRS data when FSRS is switched off or a card changes deck, and
  Set Due Date under FSRS;
- Reset Card (formerly Forget, `lib/resetCards.ts`) with Anki's two options, "Restore original
  position where possible" (on by default) and "Reset repetition and lapse counts" (off by
  default). The reviewer and the browser each remember the last choice. The card, its `cards.data`
  and its review-log row match Anki's for every combination (`lib/resetCards.test.ts`). Cards
  without a position to restore are numbered from the highest new-card position, where Anki keeps
  a separate `nextPos` counter;
- retrievability for card info, the review sort orders and `prop:r`: seconds since `lrt`, the
  card's own decay (0.5 when it has none);
- the browser's retrievability column sort and a filtered deck's gather orders
  (`lib/ankiSortKeys.ts`): Anki's `extract_fsrs_retrievability`,
  `extract_fsrs_relative_retrievability` and `fnvhash` tiebreak, computed in f32 in JS because
  SQLite has neither the functions nor 32-bit math (`lib/ankiSortKeys.test.ts`).

Full item-by-item record in `docs/AUDIT_FSRS_ANKI_PARITY.md`

**Next compatibility gate:** Known differences: memory states differ in the last bits in about 1% of cases because the platform's exp/pow
are not correctly rounded (intervals never differed in the recorded vectors); the optimizer is not
Anki's trainer and will not reproduce its numbers; preview answers write no cramming revlog row;
a filtered deck's `prop:r` compares in 64-bit SQL, which can only differ from Anki's 32-bit value
for a card within about a second of the threshold. Still to add:
retention/true-retention graphs and the FSRS simulator

## Filtered decks and custom study

**Status:** Implemented

**Evidence:** Context-preserving overview panels, two independent filters, all 11 gather search orders (0..10) stored with Anki's own ordinals (migration 10), rescheduling/preview modes whose Again/Hard/Good delays mirror Anki's `preview_again_secs`/`preview_hard_secs`/`preview_good_secs` (read from `Deck.Filtered` fields 7/5/6, which `decks.proto` numbers out of order; an omitted field imports as zero exactly as proto3 and Anki's schema-11 serde defaults leave it, and `60`/`600`/`0` are used only when creating a deck, matching `Deck::new_filtered`, whose second search term also starts at 20 cards) with Easy always retiring the card as `preview_filter.rs` does, gather orders built from `rslib/src/storage/card/filtered.rs` including its note-id/ordinal added order and its single timeline for due order, reviewer session countdown timers, excluded-card reporting, in-app help, deck manager, `lib/filteredDeckOptions.test.ts`, `lib/studyRepository.scope.test.ts`, and reviewer preview queue tests

**Gather order:** every order but random is computed in JS from Anki's own keys, including the retrievability pair and relative overdueness, which Anki takes from registered SQL functions, and the `fnvhash(c.id, c.mod)` tiebreak (`lib/filteredDeckQueue.ts`, `lib/ankiSortKeys.ts`). With FSRS off the retrievability pair keeps only the tiebreak, as upstream. Checked against a filtered deck Anki 26.05 built for each order: the same cards in the same order (`lib/ankiSortKeys.test.ts`).

**Next compatibility gate:** For a new card, the due and relative-overdueness orders read the queue position as a day number. Anki counts those days from the collection's creation and this app from 1970, so a new card whose position is beyond the collection's age in days is gathered first here and last in Anki. A filtered deck is never written to an exported package and its cards return to their home deck; upstream instead exports it as filtered when every original deck is present, and otherwise converts it to a regular deck keeping the cards inside it

## HTML, images, audio, video, TTS

**Status:** Implemented subset with an untrusted-content boundary

**Evidence:** sanitizer/CSP, card-content, media and package tests; Anki TTS language/voice/speed parsing, installed Enhanced/Premium iOS voice preference, generation-safe single speech queue, reviewer-only focus/foreground gating and hidden-content filtering are covered by focused TTS tests; scripts, network loads, active media documents and silent navigation are blocked. Reviewer and note editor both resolve Anki's bare media filenames by loading their WebView from the media directory (`lib/localMediaDocument.ts`), so a field shows its own images while its stored HTML keeps the plain filename; both documents refuse every navigation but their own base, keep file-origin reads and network off, and are pinned by `lib/localMediaDocument.test.ts` and `lib/cardContentSecurity.test.ts`. An attached file is named after the bytes actually copied rather than after the picker's label for them (`mediaFilenameForPickedAsset`, `lib/mediaFilename.test.ts`): iOS reports the library original (`IMG_0042.HEIC`) while handing over a transcoded JPEG, and storing that name would put a mislabelled media entry into the collection and into every `.apkg` exported from it; the same rule names an untouched photo saved out of the editor. The reference written into the field is built in one place and escaped for the attribute it lands in (`mediaReferenceSnippet`, `lib/mediaAttach.test.ts`), so a quote or an ampersand in a picked filename cannot close `src` early, and a bracket is taken out of an audio filename before the file is stored rather than truncating its own `[sound:…]` marker

**Next compatibility gate:** Add offline MathJax and custom-font runtime QA without weakening CSP. On web the field editor runs the same document and script as the iPhone WebView, in a same-origin iframe the host calls into directly; its media, which lives in IndexedDB there, is shown through object URLs with the real filename kept in `data-tus-src` and restored before the field is saved. The field script is one constant whose hash the web app's CSP names, because a srcdoc iframe inherits the page's policy (`lib/richTextEditorScript.ts`, `lib/webAppContentSecurityPolicy.ts` and their tests). A non-media file attachment is stored, referenced and exported, but the link policy allows `href` only for a fragment or an absolute https URL, so the reviewer renders it with `href="#"` and it does not open the file (pinned in `lib/mediaAttach.test.ts`); opening one needs the link policy and the reviewer's navigation rules changed together

## Whiteboard/scratchpad and drawing attachment

**Status:** Implemented subset

**Evidence:** geometry/photo tests, reviewer UI, and a blank drawing page that opens the full annotation editor (pen, highlighter, shapes, movable text, placed pictures, partial/whole eraser, undo/redo, crop, rotate) with ruled/plain paper pinned by `lib/blankCanvas.test.ts`; the saved PNG is attached as a normal `<img>` media reference. A picture chosen from the library or taken with the camera is placed on the page as an annotation rather than a second attachment: it lands where the page was tapped, it is drawn under the ink so every stroke made over it stays on top, it is carried through undo, crop and a quarter turn with everything else on the sheet, and it is baked into the exported PNG at the export surface's resolution. The eraser deliberately passes over it — rubbing out a stroke drawn on a picture must not take the picture with it — so a picture is removed by selecting it and using its own bin (`photoImagePlacement`, `resizePhotoImage`, `lib/photoEditor.test.ts`). A placed picture and a written label are manipulated by one selection frame, which is derived from each kind's own geometry rather than built twice (`selectionGeometryFor`, `photoSelectionHandlePoints`): its four corners resize about the corner opposite the one being dragged, keeping a picture's aspect exact and scaling a label by its font size, measured from the box the corner was grabbed at so a drag never compounds its own samples and a drag back restores the size it started from (`photoHandleResizeFactor`, `photoAnchorForFixedCorner`, `resizePhotoImageByHandle`, `resizePhotoTextByHandle`); its knob turns the object freely and settles onto quarter and half turns the finger is already within a few degrees of (`resolvePhotoHandleRotation`), and swaps to the other side of the frame rather than being clipped off a page it would hang over (`photoRotateHandleSide`); a drag gives way to the page's own centre lines, measured on the box's middle rather than on the anchor a label hangs from (`resolvePhotoDragSnap`). The handles are painted at the same points the gestures hit-test against, so a handle is never somewhere other than where it must be reached for. Either object tool takes hold of either kind of object, in the order the page draws them — a label over a picture is what the finger reached for — a second tap on a label opens it for editing, and a label written by tapping the page lands under that tap. One range governs a label's size however it is changed, by the pill, by a pinch or by a corner (`clampPhotoTextSize`). A page carries its ruling rather than re-deriving it, so cropping trims the sheet without resizing its squares and a quarter turn takes the rules with the ink — writing stays on the line it was written on, and lined paper turned on its side is ruled down the page; undo restores the sheet along with the strokes. The paper, page colour and shape a drawing was made on are remembered and reopened for the next one (`lib/blankCanvasSetup.ts`), a local row like the board's, and leaving the editor with unsaved ink asks first. The PNG is rendered on its own off-screen surface at the drawing's real resolution rather than photographed off the canvas the user drew on, so a page created at 1600×1200 is exported at 1600×1200 and an annotated photo keeps its detail up to a 2000px cap instead of collapsing to a screen's worth of pixels; stroke widths and label sizes are taken up with the surface, so the exported drawing is the one that was on screen (`photoExportSurface`, `scalePhotoAnnotation`, `lib/photoEditor.test.ts`). The reviewer board is bounded to the card stage, so ink never lands on the reviewer toolbar or the fixed answer bar, and those stay tappable while drawing. Ink clears only on a genuine move to another card (as AnkiDroid does), on a deck switch, or when the learner clears it: an empty queue is not a card change, so a learn-ahead rebuild, a timebox checkpoint or the "all done" screen no longer destroys a drawing. Auto Advance is suspended while the board is open and its dwell resumes rather than restarting, and a background queue refresh holds the drawn-on card in front; both rules are pinned by `lib/reviewerTimers.test.ts` The board's on/off state, stylus-only choice and pen colour are stored per deck and restored on return, as AnkiDroid's MetaDB `whiteboardState` row does, with a separate pen kept for the light and dark themes and non-hex colours refused before they reach the renderer (`lib/whiteboardSession.ts`, `lib/whiteboardSession.test.ts`). Like MetaDB, the row is local and stays out of the exported collection: a pen colour describes the device in the learner's hand, not the collection

**Next compatibility gate:** Apple Pencil-only mode is pending. In the classic (default) study layout the answer buttons scroll inside the card column, so they sit under the board while drawing is on; AnkiDroid keeps them in a separate bar

## Collection/database check

**Status:** Implemented subset

**Evidence:** Database: the audit and the repair share one definition of every defect (`lib/maintenance.ts`), so "Onar ve optimize et" cannot claim a fix the read-only "Veritabanını kontrol et" does not see. The button audits first, shows the counts it found, and repairs only after the learner accepts; `lib/maintenanceWorkflow.ts` puts a safety backup on disk before any row is rewritten. The repair runs as one transaction, deletes cards no note owns and notes with no cards left, graves what it deletes, rescues cards whose deck is gone (home deck first, otherwise "Kurtarılan Kartlar"), clears filtered-deck bookkeeping left on a card whose deck is not filtered — `odid` and `odue` to zero with `due` deliberately untouched, as `rslib/src/dbcheck.rs` `check_filtered_cards` does — clears a stale `odue` from a learning or review card that was never in a filtered deck (`fix_odue.sql`, zeroing branch only), folds a new card's runaway queue position back into the reserved million (`fix_due_new.sql`), pulls a review card scheduled past day 100000 back to today and rounds any other due into 32 bits (`fix_due_other.sql`), clamps a template ordinal into `[0, 30000]` (`fix_ordinal.sql`), and rounds an interval into `[0, 2147483647]` the way `fix_ivl.sql` does. Two of those rules needed adapting rather than porting, and `lib/maintenance.ts` says so at each: day numbers here count from the Unix epoch rather than the collection's creation day, so a review due today is around 20,700 and upstream's day-100000 threshold keeps its meaning only because it still lands centuries out; and a learning step is stored here in milliseconds where upstream stores seconds, so upstream's 32-bit clamp is skipped for a card mid-step, which it would otherwise move to 1970. It never deletes or moves a paid-catalog row, but it does repair a catalog card's scheduling state: a schedule the card cannot be studied on is the learner's problem, not the catalog's content; then REINDEX, ANALYZE and the search rebuild run with VACUUM last, and any step that fails is named instead of collapsing into one blanket error (`lib/maintenance.test.ts` against real SQLite, `lib/maintenanceWorkflow.test.ts`, `lib/noteManager.searchIndex.test.ts`). Media: a user-facing media check is not offered — it was removed from Data Management as a report nobody acted on. The one scanner it used to call still answers what a field, template or stylesheet refers to, and the `.apkg` exporter and the import rewriter both ask it (`extractMediaFilenames`, `rewriteMediaReferences`, `lib/mediaAttach.test.ts`), so re-adding the report is a screen, not an engine. It reads `[sound:…]`, `src`/`href`/`poster` quoted or not, and `url(…)`, and decodes the entities an attribute is written with. They used to be three patterns that disagreed: the audit did not read a note type's CSS or templates and so called a card background unused, the exporter did not read `<a href>` and so shipped a package without the file it referenced, and the import rewriter knew about neither and left a renamed file referenced under its old name

**Next compatibility gate:** Anki additionally regenerates cards a note type's templates should produce and deletes notes whose note type is gone; neither is repaired here. Of its card-property fixes, everything `check_card_properties` runs is implemented except `fix_mod` (an integer cast on a field this schema keeps in `updated_at`) and the FSRS `last_review_time` rebuild, which needs a revlog scan per card. `fix_low_ease` is deliberately absent: it belongs to saving deck options, not to the database check. Its tag-list rebuild has no counterpart because tags are derived from the notes rather than kept in a registry. A note whose JSON no longer parses is reported and left alone — its text can only come back from a backup. Revlog rows belonging to a deleted card are kept on purpose, so lifetime statistics do not shrink. The audit → confirm → repair flow now has a named iPhone smoke test in `docs/IOS_RELEASE_CHECKLIST.md`

## Shared-deck marketplace

**Status:** Product-specific catalog only

**Evidence:** BKA catalog tests. The protection is enforced where the rows are written, not by hiding buttons: `assertCatalogCardsMovable`, `assertCatalogNoteContentMutable` and `assertCatalogDeckNotDeletable` are called from `lib/noteManager.ts` and `lib/deckManager.ts` themselves, and the export functions re-check independently of the picker that filters catalog decks out. What the catalog owns is its content — the fields, the note type, the guid, the deck it lives in. What the learner owns is their own study metadata, so flags carry no assertion at all and tags are written through `setNoteTags`, a path that re-reads the stored row and swaps only the tag list, on the trial tier as well as the full one. A selection mixing personal and catalog notes tags all of them rather than rolling the batch back (`lib/catalogProtection.test.ts`, `lib/noteManager.catalogTags.test.ts`)

**Next compatibility gate:** Do not present it as AnkiWeb shared decks
